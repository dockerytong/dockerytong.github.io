Reactive mineral interfaces as transfer functions

Assistant Researcher, Research Center for Eco-Environmental Sciences (RCEES), Chinese Academy of Sciences Beijing, China

My work asks how reactive mineral interfaces form and transform, and how those processes change the fate of carbon, metals, and microorganisms. The working idea is that the interface behaves as a transfer function: redox fluctuation, water and oxygen ingress, light, and pH enter as forcing, and the interface converts them into outcomes that persist — carbon stabilization, metal immobilization, or an electron supply that microbes can use. Establishing that transfer function comes first; once it is established, the question becomes whether it can be driven deliberately.

Electrochemistry is where both halves of that question meet. The same electrode reads electron flux and imposes potential, so a mineral–water interface can be observed and perturbed on the same device. I combine in-operando electrochemistry with density functional theory, in situ and synchrotron-based characterization, and microbial community analysis, and I keep the atomistic side (DFT, machine-learned potentials) calibrated against experiment rather than used as decoration.

I did my PhD with Chuanping Feng at China University of Geosciences (Beijing) on pyrite oxidation under neutral conditions and its use to drive autotrophic denitrification, then spent two years as a Boya postdoc with Anhuai Lu at Peking University on mineral photocatalysis and iron cycling in the euphotic zone. Since 2025 I have been at RCEES, where the interface question is carried into tailings-to-soil formation and organo-mineral coupling.

中文 我的研究关注反应性矿物界面如何形成与转化，以及这些过程如何改变碳、金属与微生物过程的归趋。 我把矿物界面当作一条传递函数：氧化还原波动、水氧入渗、光照与 pH 作为输入，界面把它们转换成 能长期保存的结果——碳稳定化、金属固持、可供微生物利用的电子供给；再用（原位）电化学把这条 传递函数拨动起来。当前体系包括黄铁矿与半导体矿物、透光层铁循环、尾矿成土与矿物—有机质耦合。

Where I work

The group is led by Songlin Wu at RCEES and works on mycorrhiza–mineral–heavy metal interfaces and tailings-to-soil formation. I am responsible for the computational and electrochemical side across the group's projects, and I am the person to contact about atomistic modelling, DFT calibration, or machine-learned interatomic potentials in a mineral-interface context.
