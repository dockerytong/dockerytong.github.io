Mineral interfaces and environmental biogeochemistry

Assistant Professor, Research Center for Eco-Environmental Sciences (RCEES), Chinese Academy of Sciences Beijing, China

I work on reactive mineral interfaces in soil, water, and mining-impacted environments: how they form and transform, and how they shape the fate of carbon, metals, and microbial processes. My current systems are iron minerals and semiconductor minerals, organo–mineral coupling, and tailings-to-soil formation.

My route crosses fields that are usually kept separate — environmental engineering, mineralogy, electrochemistry, and computational chemistry; the techniques and their honest limits are on the Methods page. That integration, not any single technique, is the capability I bring to a collaboration.

I did my PhD with Chuanping Feng at China University of Geosciences (Beijing) on pyrite oxidation under neutral conditions and its use to drive autotrophic denitrification, then spent two years as a Boya postdoc with Anhuai Lu at Peking University on mineral photocatalysis and iron cycling in the euphotic zone. Since 2025 I have been at RCEES, where the interface question is carried into tailings-to-soil formation and organo-mineral coupling.

Where I work and collaborate

The group is led by Songlin Wu at RCEES and works on mycorrhiza–mineral–heavy metal interfaces and tailings-to-soil formation. Across the group's projects I manage the geochemical measurement side — synchrotron spectroscopy method development, hyperspectral data-analysis pipelines, and electrochemistry — alongside the computational work (atomistic modelling, DFT calibration, and machine-learned potentials). Microbiology has dedicated members in the group; the omics analyses attached to my questions are mine.

For external collaborations on mineral–water or mineral–microbe interfaces, I typically contribute on three fronts: (1) in-operando electrochemical measurements coupled with synchrotron speciation (XAS, STXM), (2) spatially resolved hyperspectral imaging and spectral-unmixing pipelines, and (3) DFT and machine-learned potential modelling anchored to measured potentials and spectroscopy. Direct inquiries on any of these are welcome at tongliu@rcees.ac.cn.
