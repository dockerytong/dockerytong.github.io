Methods

My route crosses fields that are usually kept separate — environmental engineering, mineralogy, electrochemistry, and computational chemistry. The integration is the capability; the sections below are where it shows up in practice.

Electrochemistry

The core technique: cyclic voltammetry, impedance including dynamic EIS during reaction, chronoamperometry, and in-operando cells that follow a mineral surface while it reacts.

Synchrotron spectroscopy and speciation

Structure and speciation from synchrotron XAS, XPS, and Raman and infrared spectroscopy. I develop the measurement methods behind this work, and the geochemical characterization across the group's projects is managed on this side.

Hyperspectral data analysis

Currently the main development effort: software and data-analysis pipelines that turn hyperspectral measurements into analysis-ready geochemical information.

Computation

DFT for adsorption, lattice response, and electron-transfer paths (VASP, ABACUS, Quantum ESPRESSO, CP2K; ORCA and Gaussian for molecular and cluster systems), with conformer and cluster search from xTB and ABCluster and ASE-based workflow automation. Machine-learned interatomic potentials (MACE) are used for sampling and screening.

On machine-learned potentials: I use them for ranking and trends, not for absolute energetics at metal–oxide interfaces, and I check the geometry they produce before believing an energy. Details in Notes.

Microbial community analysis

Amplicon and metagenomic sequencing. The group has dedicated microbiology members; the omics analyses attached to my own questions stay with me.
